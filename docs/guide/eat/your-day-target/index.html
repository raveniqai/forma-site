Your day target, and why it moves

How the calorie and protein target is worked out each day.

On this page
  • When to use it
  • Step by step
  • A worked example
  • Good to know
  • Related

Your day target is the calories — and the protein, carbs and fat — your body is expected to use today, adjusted for your goal. It moves on purpose: a training day is weighted for the training, and it follows your body and your recent logging. You never type the number yourself.

When to use it

  • You want to know what the ring on Fuel is counting down.
  • The target changed in the last few days and you want the reason.
  • It reads far too high or too low, and you want to check the inputs.

Step by step

  1. On Fuel, look at the big ring: the calories left today, or the calories over once you are past the target.
  2. Tap the ring, or tap Energy & target at the bottom of Fuel.
  3. Read Why this number: one plain sentence about what you burn on a day like this and where your goal takes it. On a training day, the line also names the rest-day figure. When the target moves because the day type did, it is recorded under Coach decisions.
  4. Under How today's target is set, the rows add up to Target: Resting burn + everyday movement, then Exercise (or Exercise (none today)), then Your deficit, Your surplus or Your pace (maintain). A Weekly nudge row appears once the weekly check has moved the base.
  5. To change your goal, tap Change my goal. It opens Settings > Profile & goal.

Below, Where you stand compares the day eaten with the target, and Is logging adding up? compares what your logging predicts with what the scale did: days you closed count, today never does.

How the number is made

The target is worked out from plain, tested arithmetic on your phone. Each part:

  • Resting burn comes from a standard formula (Mifflin-St Jeor), using your height, birth date and sex in Settings > Profile & goal and the 7-day average of your weigh-ins. A lean-mass reading replaces the formula when you have one.
  • Everyday movement adds 10 percent of the resting burn: the everyday part, before any exercise.
  • Exercise adds a typical session's cost: 250 kcal on a strength day, 350 on a run day, until Forma has at least 3 of your own sessions to take the middle of. A day not marked as a rest day counts that session from the start, before you log it. A day marked as a rest day adds nothing. Past 21:00, a day with no session logged counts as a rest day. Once your watch reports today's session, its figure replaces the estimate.
  • Your goal moves the day by one fixed amount of calories from your average day: about 17.5 percent off to cut, nothing to hold, 12.5 percent on to eat up. On a cut with a body-fat reading, the deficit that reading gives is used instead of the percentage. The goal itself is decided from your measured body fat (see Body fat and lean mass); until you have one, you choose it in Settings > Profile & goal.
  • The weekly check may move the base of the target once a week, and only with at least 10 days of weight trend and 10 days of logged food to judge. On its own it never moves the target more than 7 percent away from what it was a week ago. It never applies a change that means losing more than 1 percent of your bodyweight a week; it asks you instead. It never takes the base below your resting burn. Its changes appear under Coach decisions (History & reports, the clock button on Progress), with an Undo.
  • A floor. The target never goes below your resting burn, or below the common least-to-eat guideline for the sex in your profile: 1,200 kcal a day for women, 1,500 for men. That guideline never lifts the target above what the day burns. When the floor holds the target up, Why this number says so.
  • The macros. Protein is a fixed amount per kilogram of your 7-day average weight: 2.1 g per kg to cut, 1.8 to hold, 1.7 to eat up. Fat is 27.5 percent of the calories; carbs fill what is left. So only carbs and fat move between a rest day and a training day; protein does not.

A worked example

Amira is 34, weighs 62 kg, is 165 cm tall. Her goal is to hold her weight, and she trains 3 days a week.

InputValue
Resting burn1,320 kcal
Everyday movement10 percent of the resting burn
Goal offset0 (holding)
Strength-day exercise250 kcal
DayCaloriesProteinCarbsFat
Rest day1,452 kcal112 g152 g44 g
Strength day1,702 kcal112 g197 g52 g

Forma's own wording for that strength day: "Strength day: 1,702 kcal — rest days are 1,452."

The 250 extra kcal on the training day do not change the protein — it stays at 112 g. The training-day extra goes to carbs and fat.

Good to know

Note: The target follows your 7-day average weight, not a single weigh-in. If a number looks off, check the weigh-ins: one typing slip, such as 780 instead of 78.0, moves the average. With no weigh-in in the last 7 days there is no target at all, and Fuel says so. Fix a reading under All weigh-ins on Progress.

Note: With the diet coach on alone, there is no training split: the target uses a typical amount of activity and is the same every day.

Limit: The floor is not optional. For Sam, a man of 88 kg set to cut, the target on a rest day is 1,820 kcal — the same as his resting burn — because the cut would otherwise have taken it under that.

Note: Protein is a floor: clearing it is the win, and going over is never counted against you. Calories, carbs and fat are budgets: over is the one to avoid. The number counts what you are over by, from the first calorie past the target. The colour turns amber only once you are more than 100 kcal past it.

Tip: Is it much too high or too low? Check the height and birth date in Settings > Profile & goal, and your recent weigh-ins — a wrong birth date or height shifts every number. A rest day being lower is by design. Then see Troubleshooting.

A past day keeps the target it had that day; today is worked out again as your weight, your day type and your watch data change. You never type a calorie number. Forma works the target out, and the goal — cut, hold, eat up — is what you change.
